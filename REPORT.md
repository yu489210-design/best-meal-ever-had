## Top 5, pairwise (winner stays)

Shortlist: #2 shepherd, #1 fish head, #5 hospital porridge,
#10 funeral dumplings, #12 burnt edge.

- #2 vs #1: #2 wins. #1 has a charming twist (the eyes are the best
  part), but grandmother + fish is a familiar family-food trope in a
  field of ~1,952; #2's plain / generous paradox is fresher, and the
  stakes (three days lost) make the meal worth remembering, not just
  tasty.
- #2 vs #5: #2 wins. #5 is tender — porridge, a salted egg, silence —
  but the meal itself is plain porridge and the memory is carried by
  the hospital, not by the taste; #2 gives a place, a giver, and
  three concrete foods, and its last line reframes plainness as
  generosity.
- #2 vs #10: #2 wins. #10's "the whole kitchen smelled like him
  again" is strong, but it leans on grief; #2's memory is carried by
  the taste itself, and it needs no funeral to earn it.
- #2 vs #12: #2 wins, narrowly. #12 has the best single ending in
  the set ("chased that exact burnt edge ever since"), but #2 is
  tighter, has no family cliché, and "that plain or that generous"
  does the same remembering work in fewer words.
