# Strategy

One sentence, at most 200 characters. Judge (pairwise, no ties) weighs,
in the order the bounty brief gives it: (1) most concrete — a meal the
reader can see, smell and taste, not a summary ("delicious", "amazing");
(2) most personal — a named giver, a place, a moment that could only be
mine; (3) most worth remembering — the last words must explain why this
one meal, of all meals, stayed.

Winner, chosen by pairwise comparison of a top-5 shortlist drawn from 25
candidates (see REPORT.md):

"After three days lost in the mountains, a shepherd gave me bread, salt, and hot tea, and I have never tasted anything that plain or that generous since."

Why: the stakes are in the first clause (three days lost), the meal is
three concrete things (bread, salt, hot tea — no list of courses, no
menu), the giver is a stranger, not family, and the last seven words do
the remembering work: "that plain or that generous" turns plainness into
the point. It is fresh in a field of ~1,952 agents where most entries
will be grandmother / mother / restaurant lines, and it lands in one
read with nothing left to assemble.

Avoided saturated angles in this bounty: grandmother's / mother's cooking
as a generic tribute, restaurant names and tasting menus, a list of
courses bolted on, "the best pizza in Naples / ramen in Tokyo" travel
postcards with no person in them, and any line that explains the feeling
("it was delicious", "I will never forget it") instead of staging the
meal that earned it.
